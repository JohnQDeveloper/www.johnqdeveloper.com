# Chapter 1: Dying Hurts the First Time

I was a fool who did not believe in the legend that was Truck-kun.

So, when a pickup truck came hurtling through a stop sign at a girl I thought I could push her out of the way and save myself. I mean, what sort of self-respecting man would let a sixteen year old die in front of him?

I shoved her out of the way only for the truck to swerve with preternatural grace and strike us both.

Dead in an instant, or at least that is what I tell people when they ask.

The reality was I lay on bloodied cement, broken and crying from the pain, on the sidewalk for awhile. Someone called 911 of course, who wouldn't call someone to save someone? Cell phones were a life saver that way, in theory.

I bled out or choked to death on my own blood or whatever before the ambulance arrived.

The truth was Truck-kun was real and no lowly mortal was going to save its victim. Certainly not a nineteen year old college student like myself.

And so, Truck-kun claimed two lives instead of one.

No one ever found the truck or truck driver. Because there was no justice in real life. Or maybe it really was the mystical Truck-kun who did the deed and not the disgruntled ex-boyfriend who was a known car thief.

Anyway. Thanks for coming to my Ted Talk.

My eyes opened as my soul once more inhabited young human flesh and I stared upon a lake filled with floating orbs showing off memories like they were YouTube. They floated on the water towards a slow moving, single file stream of souls that headed towards golden, ornate gates that stood closed against the tide.

An angelic being could be seen at the end of the stream, writing down something in his notebook before making the next soul in line disappear in a flash of light.

Where did they go?

Was this Heaven?

"We are at the Gates of the High Heavens, to pass beyond is to be forever closed to reincarnation and be granted one's eternal reward. But I think at nineteen, that is a little early for you to being calling it quits on writing your life story, hmmm?"

I turned around to find a large chested woman who was easily over six feet tall. An impressive stature, for sure, as she loomed over me and continued, "So, what are we do with you hmmm? Died too young to have the karmic credits to be let in but died in such an honorable way. Most people just let a murder victim die to save themselves."

I stared up into the celestial orbs that were eyes, like stars in the voids of the night sky. "Who are you, ma'am?"

She smiled, "I am Hestia. An old god bound to serve the Goddess of the High Heavens and her flock of angels."

"Are you doing something to keep me calm? I should be freaking out. I'm dead. I should feel something besides calm."

She shrugged. "You can be recycled, as the Cycle does these things, to try again or admitted into the Forerunner Academy with your memories intact. I would not wish for your grief over your death to decide the matter since Forerunners often die very permanent deaths at the hands of Odium's monstrosities. It would be cruel to throw you into the fires of war just because you were emotional."

I looked around. There was no war here. Pearly, golden gates. Angels in the sky. Souls in a lake. Certainly a place of death and rebirth but war? It seemed so peaceful.

"The High Heavens is far from the front lines. A retirement home of heroes and heroines who did their duty for the cause. People who, in many ways, are so broken by the War they want to forget but no longer can. So they go past the pearly gates to rest. Perhaps they will sally forth again, perhaps not. But at least for a time, there is peace."

I turned back to her. "What war? If it was not the fact it sounds like I'd be a soldier, I'd seriously consider it. I don't really want to die for good."

She shrugged. "Some people feel a ship, with its planks replaced but in the same shape, is still the same ship. Some do not. Reincarnation is like that. You forget much, yes, but you are still the same shape and perhaps luck will let you have a full life instead of one cut tragically short."

"As for the war? Well, Odium believes that the Qi of a person's soul guarentees the immortality of the gods. So why let mortals make use of it? Why not take it and grow stronger? Live longer?

No matter what anyone tells you the lives of gods are finite. It takes a full soul's worth of that vital energy to extend our lifespan a hundred years just like it does for you.

Hence, war. The Heavens, the Cycle, even demons do not believe wiping out mortals is the correct choice. You reproduce, sometimes creating new souls, and siphoning off a year or there is much more humane. Even the deals demons make with people allow for a mortal to sell their soul. They cannot be forced into it for the magic to take hold."

I stared at her. It was alot to take in, yet my brain was filled with more than her words. Tidbits of memories. Time as a champion of the Cycle, a cultivator, who lived a thousand years. Another time as a demon, making deals to keep his loved ones safe. The memories were fleeting but in every one I was someone of importance, not simply a boy who died saving a little girl.

"And now, the mind understands its past as well as its present. The process is never complete, never sharing all that came before. However, you have never served us so we cannot admit you even though your soul came to us. You see the issue? You sacrificed yourself, as a hero would, and yet we cannot let you in. The karmic debts from your past lives are simply too high."

"That is unfair. To be punished for lives I could not even remember!"

She sighed. "Mortals feel that way but as I said, the soul always takes the same shape in the end. Yours is one that sides with those he cares about over strangers. Sure, you might sacrifice yourself but not those you love. And that leads you into the same cycle of trading in souls for power to protect them. Perhaps this time, knowing the shape, you might change it and prove worthy. But only if you enter the Academy.

If not, you will simply forget again."

This was bullshit! BULLSHIT!

I gave up my life trying to save another and I get told I don't deserve heaven because of some past me, that I don't even really remember, did something bad.

"I agree with you but I don't make the rules. Even the Goddess only has a say in such matters. The demons always want a chance to reclaim their own. The Cycle believes in reincarnation, yes, but also in the debts that follow the soul. So two against one, and the motion carried into law that even a goddess cannot break. It simply is how reality works now."

I stared at her as my mind fought the calming influence until I was once again calm. A young man, even one with many past lives as someone of some influence, was nothing before a literal goddess. Even one bound into servitude.

She continued, "So remember this conversation and all that you do right now and enter the Academy. Or be blissfully unaware and try to crawl out of the heap that is humanity to reach the Heavens. The choice is yours but you must choose. I may be a goddess but there are many souls and the fires of war demand constant tending. I would coddle you and give you years to decide and train if I could. But the truth is, we are losing slowly but losing all the same. Each hour that passes millions of souls are lost to the maws of Odium's monsters from which he takes his tithe and grows stronger still. Sooner or later he'll snuff out the weakest of us, the Cycle, and then it will be over quickly."

Logically, her words led to only one conclusion. If defeat was inevitable if nothing changed, one might as well fight and hope for the best. One more soldier might not make a difference but one of many soldiers making that decision would.

I am not sure still if she made me make this choice the way she wanted or if I was truly persuaded. It was all too fast for such a life altering decision with so much new information. Yet I said, "I will join the Academy," anyway for reasons I still do not understand to this day.

She smiled and I woke up in a lecture hall with marble floors, comfortable leather recliners, and a few hundred students already seated. Just like I was.
