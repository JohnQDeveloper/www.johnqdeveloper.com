## Chapter 1: Integration Day

I stared at the glowing writing in front of my eyes.

**Reality distorts. Time bends. Space contorts. The gods have arrived and the war for souls begins.**

**Please prompt me, as you would an AI, so that we may make your class selection together.**

My mind wandered back to my teenage years when I met Natasha.

I was walking my dog Buddy when the night sky turned red as a house fire spread to the roof and a young woman, an exchange student no one knew anything about at the time, rushed into the building in a blur of movement that already seemed off somehow. How could a teenage girl move faster than an olympic sprinter?

Buddy barked as we ran toward the scene, dialing 911 on my cell phone in the hopes that anybody, somebody would save the teenage beauty that ran into a house on fire!

The call was hardly memorable, just robotic instructions and responses about the location and the crisis that required the firefighters.

But by the time I reached the house and could see inside, the door had fallen in as if it had been ripped off the hinges. The young woman had an unconsious man, Mr. Jameson the math teacher, in a fireman's carry as if she had done this before. But what sixteen year old girl would have learned such a thing?

A rumble echoed in the street as two-by-fours fell as part of the roof and attic failed and I screamed. She was about to be buried and the world would be poorer for it.

Yet, they simply rebounded off of an invisible barrier a couple feet from the pair in the burning house.

I was convinced I had taken some good drugs and just forgotten when I saw that. There was no way that could have happened as she exited the building, dropped the man on his law, and bolted when she saw me.

The rest of the night was a blur and I hardly knew what to say when I was asked if anyone was inside. As far as I knew, the math teacher lived alone, and would such a fearless heroine really abandon someone in there? Doubtful, so I told them he stumbled outside and collapsed after muttering something about living alone. It was clearly bullshit. The firefighter's look said as much but that was fine.

I was fine. The secret I now shared with the mysterious exchange student remained a secret. After all, superheroes who forgot their mask at home needed people to keep their identity secret, right?

---

I was awake all night running through scenarios in my head about how to safely confront her. I did not even remember the young lady's name, just her face when she was introduced around mid-semester. Who could forget the face of a woman so beautiful?

I was exhausted with only caffeine pills to keep me going throughout the day, I had a couple classes with her but what was I going to say in front of others? "Hey, I saw you save the math teacher that is missing today becuase his house burned down?"

So being the introvert that I was I followed her as she left her last class. She glanced over her shoulder a couple times and clearly recognized me but with the mass of students neither of us wanted to say anything as everyone left for home at the end of the school day.

No one wanted to stay at the high school a second longer than they had to. It was why detention worked as a deterrent, no one wanted to finish a day then spend another hour in that place.

As the crowd thinned and we both turned down the same side street she paused and stared at me. The fact I was following her at this point was obvious to both of us but she clearly suspect it was more than a high school crush by the serious look on her face.

I approached her calmly and said softly, not to be overheard by passers by on the other side of the residential street, "I saw you last night. With the math teacher."

It was casual enough I thought to not sound like an accusation even if it sounded more like they were sleeping together than she saved the man's life.

She stared, eyes bored into my soul, "I knew you did. I appreciate you not blabbing. If there is nothing else?"

He sighed and said, "How did you stop the falling lumber when the roof fell?"

She sighed and looked away, her lovely brown eyes hidden for the moment. "I wish you had forgotten that part. I don't want to spend the rest of my time here grounded by my parents, ok?"

I smiled. At least she was not insulting my intelligence or mind controlling me or whatever it was I had feared the night before. "So, care to talk about it?"

She gestured towards the park down the street on the way to what appeared to be both our houses and I started walking. My body was abuzz with excitement, a real life super hero going to my school! Would I be her side kick? Her confidante? Her friend? It was the first real adventure of my life.

The park was deserted at a little after 2pm. High schoolers were either into sports or hated exercise as a general rule. So why take the scenic route home when the residential street was the direct route?

Once we were truly alone she said, "We are here because Earth is slated to be integrated in the next couple of decades. The wider multiverse, for better or worse, is coming to you. I'm simply here to assess Earth culture for first contact."

First contact? She was an alien superhero like superman!?

I stared at her only for her to take a few more steps before she realized I had stopped walking.

She looked at him, "Look, you can't tell anyone about what you saw okay? They will put you in one of those asylums or worse, believe you and the government will toss you in some hole for observation. You don't want that."

It took me a moment to catch up and I knew she was right. Nothing ever good came of unmasking a super hero or an alien. It was always some government conspiracy or villain conspiracy ruining their lives after that.

I nodded. "Sure, I can keep a secret. But why Earth? What is integration?"

She sighed and we walked side by side for awhile, "Well, there is a war. Some say it is cold but for a newly integrated world it will be a hot one. The System acts as a referee, prevents the worst of the worst from happening to people to a degree. Before the System, entire worlds were cracked and left lifeless. One side would genocide the other sides followers and so on. Only Odium still finds ways to bend the rules and wipe out entire civilizations sometimes. Exploits they call it."

I paused at the idea of some being like Galactus wiping out entire planets. Odium? What did that even mean? I would find out later it meant hatred and disgust when I looked it up and that helped explain the context. Odium hated everyone else simply for existing. They were disgusted by the idea of mere mortals being a thing.

She continued, "So, it is coming. I would prepare you but the truth is I'll have to report this to my parents no matter what I want. We'll be gone tomorrow and forgotten by most."

She shrugged and offered me what looked to be a small ring she had on her finger, "Its a communication device for when it happens. If you keep it secret, I'll contact you when the time comes. All I can suggest is you prepare as best you can with what is available once you graduate. You'll have a few years, maybe even close to two decades, to prepare. Then everything will go to shit."

If it was not for the fact I had seen literally flaming rubble bounce off her personal shield, I would have thought she was some nutty apocalypse preacher. But the fact I had? Well, that made things very different.

She chuckled and gave me a quick kiss on the cheek, "Take care, James. I truly am sorry Integration is coming to Earth. But we can't stop it, no one can as long as the Gods will the war to continue. And I doubt Odium will ever stop waging it against the others."

She was gone a moment later. Not simply walked away but vanished without a trace.

---

I found out later her name was Natasha when I read the inscription inside the ring. I don't know why I never asked, it just seemed to important to find out her secrets at the time I supposed.

Everyone forgot her. No one mentioned the fact she was no longer in class. They did not even call her name or mark her absent. It was as if she was never there. Even my parents forgot the beautiful exchange student I had mentioned when she first arrived.

I am not sure if it was the ring that I wore for the rest of my life that allowed me to remember or if she had done something to allow me to keep my memories but I appreciated it.

I realized as I got older she pitied me. I never truly understood why until today. Integration was real. The war would burn hot and it was at my doorstep. There was no running. No hiding. No safe place to bunker down.

Sure, I had prepared for this day despite my mom calling me a crazy prepper when we moved to Hayden after my father's death, but still. A part of me always hoped this day would never come.
